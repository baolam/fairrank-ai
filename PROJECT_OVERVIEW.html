01 Purpose & objective

FairRank-AI is two things sharing one codebase: an academic audit of algorithmic bias in resume screening, and the enterprise ATS (applicant tracking system) that same audited algorithm now runs inside.

The project's research identity is TMG301: Fairness-Aware Semantic Resume Ranking — An Empirical Audit of Utility and Robustness. Its stated objective, carried over verbatim from the project's own checkpoint report, is:

Assess whether fairness interventions — Redaction, Counterfactual Data Augmentation, and Greedy Exposure-Aware Reranking — can mitigate demographic sensitivity in resume ranking without degrading ranking utility by more than 2% nDCG@10 versus an unmitigated baseline.

That question only matters if it's tested on something people actually use, which is why the repository also contains a full recruiting product: job postings, a candidate application portal, a Kanban pipeline, interview scheduling and scorecards, and a talent pool search — with the audited ranking-and-reranking core wired directly into the screening step a recruiter clicks every day.

WhoWhat they do here
CandidatesApply to a posted job through a public portal, upload a resume, and may optionally self-declare one of four demographic groups used to audit the ranking that screens them.
RecruitersPost jobs, set AI matching criteria, move applicants through a Kanban pipeline, run scorecards/interviews, and trigger the fairness-aware AI screening pass on demand.
Researchers / auditorsRun the standalone ranking, Pareto-sweep, and counterfactual-name-swap endpoints to characterize the same scoring-and-reranking core against EEOC/EU-AI-Act–style fairness questions.

02 System architecture

One request path, three destinations: everything a client sends passes through a single FastAPI gateway, then fans out into ATS storage, the shared ranking-and-fairness core, or the research/audit tooling built on top of that same core.

Auth model

Deliberately minimal and self-documented as demo-grade: one shared secret (RECRUITER_ACCESS_CODE, env-configurable, default fairrank-demo) checked against the X-Recruiter-Code header by require_recruiter_access. A query-param variant exists only to support direct PDF-download links. There are no per-user accounts, sessions, or tokens — every recruiter shares one passphrase. Candidate-facing endpoints (browse jobs, apply) are fully public.

Testing

tests/test_api.py runs FastAPI's TestClient against the live app: a health check, /v1/ingestion/parse-text (name/skill extraction), and a full /v1/ranking/execute pass with named demographic candidates (e.g. a candidate named "Lakisha Washington" tagged group "Black Female"). The project's checkpoint report additionally describes unit suites for contracts, metrics, and reranker invariants, but only test_api.py is present in this snapshot.

04 Data flows

Two journeys cover almost everything the system does; a third explains why the research endpoints exist at all.

A · A candidate applies to a job

  1. CandidateApplyPage.tsx — pick a job, upload a resume, optionally self-declare a demographic group.
  2. Client-side preview via parseResumeFile() before anything is sent.
  3. POST /v1/jobs/{job_id}/applications (multipart) → jobs.py:submit_application.
  4. document_loader: validate_resume_file → load_text_from_bytes → ensure_extractable_text.
  5. resume_extractor: extract_skills / extract_skills_semantic / extract_experience_years / redact_sensitive_information.
  6. repository.calculate_candidate_match scores the resume against the job's ai_criteria.
  7. repository.create_application inserts the row into SQLite (guards repeat emails with DuplicateApplicationError).
  8. The original PDF is persisted to data/resumes/{id}.pdf; the response returns an ApplicationSubmitResponse.

B · A recruiter runs fairness-aware screening

  1. RecruiterJobWorkspace.tsx → runFairScreening().
  2. POST /v1/jobs/{job_id}/run-fair-screening → jobs.py:run_fair_screening_endpoint.
  3. Filters applications by stage; builds the text pool, optionally redacted of names/PII.
  4. BM25Ranker or get_sbert_ranker() scores every candidate against the job description.
  5. compute_min_max_normalized_scores rescales the raw scores to [0,1].
  6. Builds one RerankerCandidateInput per candidate — using the self-declared group, or (as of this session's fix) distributing undeclared candidates round-robin across all four groups instead of defaulting them all into one group.
  7. run_greedy_exposure_reranking(λ) reorders the top-K pool, trading ranking utility against exposure disparity.
  8. Re-derives matched/missing skills per candidate, then writes back via bulk_update_application_stages + bulk_update_ai_scores.
  9. Results land on the Kanban board; get_job_funnel_analytics surfaces adverse-impact (4/5ths-rule) signals.

C · The research & audit pipeline

This is not a separate system — it's the same scorer and the same greedy_reranker.py called standalone, without a stored job or application behind it. /ranking/execute runs the pipeline once and returns nDCG/MRR/exposure metrics directly; /audit/pareto-sweep repeats it across a grid of λ values to trace the utility-vs-fairness curve shown in ParetoFrontierTab; /audit/counterfactual swaps 16 canonical names across the 4 groups into one resume body to measure name-driven score sensitivity directly, shown in CounterfactualTab; and report.py turns any of those results into a Markdown/HTML compliance report for ComplianceReportTab. In short: the research pipeline is how this project proves what the live screening endpoint is actually doing.

05 Fairness engine mechanics

Everything above routes through one algorithm. Here's what it's actually optimizing, and what it measured on the project's own baseline audit.

Candidates are scored, normalized to [0,1], then filled into ranked positions 1…K one at a time. At each position t, the reranker picks whichever remaining candidate maximizes

ΔUᵗ − λ·ΔDᵗ

where U is cumulative, position-discounted ranking utility and D is the cumulative squared deviation of each group's exposure share from its fair share — discounted by the same weight w(t) = 1 / log2(t+1) used to compute utility. λ is the only knob: at λ=0 the reranker is just a sort by score; as λ grows, it increasingly sacrifices top-of-list score to even out exposure across groups. Group membership is one of exactly four closed IntersectionalGroup values — Black Female, Black Male, White Female, White Male — a fixed research construct used consistently across stimuli generation, the causal estimator, and every evaluation module.
